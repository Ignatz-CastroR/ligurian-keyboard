# 0038: The senses reviewed one by one

* **Status:** accepted
* **Date:** 2026/10/20
* **Decided by:** Eng. José Ignacio Castro de R.
* **Refines:** [0011](0011-roles-and-review-workflow.md), on a reviewer's power to soft delete senses and translations while regrouping them

## Context

Before 0.12.1 brought `db/templates/add-entries.sql`, words were loaded by SQL in a shape the dictionary does not intend: one sense per translation, and every translation written as Italian. `db/templates/check-loaded-entries.sql`, run on 2026/10/20, measured what that left in the live dictionary: 5,731 lemmas whose 15,300 senses look split one per translation [several senses, none with a definition, each with exactly one translation], and 7 translations holding ";", several translations in one.

The obvious repair is a bulk merge: one sense per lemma. A speaker judged a sample of 25 of those lemmas, drawn at random [seed 20261020]: 12 are one meaning, 8 are right as they are, 5 need a regrouping of their own [for example `ségno`, whose seven translations make five senses]. A bulk merge would be right for about 48% of them [95% interval roughly 30% to 68%], and any other default is wrong just as often. Five chosen entries, `stécco`, `sacrificâ`, `sachezâ`, `sachézzo` and `gabìbbo`, showed every kind: two senses kept apart, two merged into one of three, three into one.

No rule can tell those cases apart; a person can, quickly, if the tool asks only that.

## Decision

* **Every lemma is reviewed by a person, one at a time.** No bulk merge, no default grouping.
* **The list is frozen when migration 0012 runs.** It marks `lemmas.senses_review_needed` on every live lemma in the shape the check measures: two or more live senses, none with a definition, each with exactly one live translation. Afterwards only the trusted backend changes the mark. A list drawn from the shape at every reading was the first design; an independent review showed that it lost a lemma as soon as anyone added a translation or a definition to it, a contributor's pending proposal or a correction made from the repair page itself included, and that it gained every new entry of the same shape. The marking is an ordinary update of each lemma, raising its version and writing a line of history, as 0006 did for the public codes; the Users section reads it as "messo fra i significati da rivedere".
* **The lemmas waiting** are those on the list, live and not yet reviewed. `sense_review_queue()` lists them fewest senses first, so that the quickest decisions come first, then by search key in byte order, then by id, in pages by key, each with its position and the number waiting.
* **A lemma reviewed is marked:** `lemmas.senses_reviewed_at`, the time a reviewer last confirmed its grouping, set by the database's clock, never by a client's. Only reviewers, admins, the trusted backend and a restore may set it; a contributor's insert has it cleared, a contributor's change of it is refused. Clearing it sends a lemma on the list back to the queue. Every change writes its line of history, which the Users section reads as "significati rivisti".
* **A regrouping is one call, whole or not at all:** `regroup_senses(lemma, seen, groups)`. `seen` names the lemma and every live sense and translation at the version the reviewer saw; anything different now, the lemma's own version included, refuses the call [40001]. So a second reviewer working from the same page is refused even after the first chose "leave as it is", which changes no sense but marks the lemma. A change made meanwhile is always reported as one, before any other check. `groups` lists the new senses in their new order, each with its translations and, when one was written, an Italian definition; every live translation must appear exactly once. A lemma on the list with no sense left is closed with no group at all.
* **What is kept and what is new.** A group holding exactly the translations of an existing sense keeps that sense, its id, author and history, renumbered if needed through a temporary number above every number in use, so that no two live senses ever share a number. Any other group becomes a new sense: a translation never moves to another sense [0002], so its translations are copied in, with their language, text and status, and the originals are soft deleted. Translations spelled alike in one language within one group are copied once, the best reviewed of them: an approved copy is never replaced by a rejected one. A new sense is approved only when every translation in it came from an approved sense. A group sent without a definition keeps the definition of the sense it keeps; a new sense then has none. The lemma is marked reviewed even when nothing changes.
* **Reviewers may soft delete, here only.** Until now only admins could soft delete [0011]. A regrouping must soft delete the senses and translations it replaces, and reviewers do the reviewing. `regroup_senses()` raises a flag for its own transaction, as `restore_backup()` does [0031]; under it the update trigger lets a reviewer soft delete senses and translations, never restore them, never touch a lemma or a form. No API request can set the flag, and it counts only for staff. Restoring stays an admin's power.
* **Refused:** a lemma with a live sense that has no translation [it would vanish without being seen], more than 100 senses, a malformed request, a caller who is not a reviewer, an admin or the trusted backend.
* **The manager:** a tab "Significati da rivedere" with the queue, and a page per lemma, `#/repair/<id>`, listing its translations with their current sense and a choice of new sense, an Italian definition field per new sense, and three actions, each on a key so that thousands of lemmas can be reviewed from the keyboard: **1** one sense, **2** leave as it is, **3** group as chosen. Each saves at once and opens the next lemma, or the first still waiting when none follows. A definition field belongs to its group of translations: it shows what was typed for it, or else the definition of the sense holding exactly those translations, it is labelled with the number the sense will have, and what it shows is what is sent. A definition shown in a field the chosen action would not use stops the action, so that no definition is lost by a key press. While a menu has the focus the keys choose in it, as a keyboard user expects. A link leads to the entry, where a translation's text or language is corrected.
* **Translations holding ";"** are split by `split_semicolon_glosses(apply)`, admins and the trusted backend only: a dry run lists each such translation and its pieces, and applying it inserts each piece in the same sense with the same language and status, then soft deletes the original. Pieces are written as the manager's own field writes them: NFC, inner spaces collapsed to one. A lemma on the list stays on it: its reviewer then sees the pieces as translations of one sense. `db/templates/split-semicolon-glosses.sql` runs it from the SQL Editor, as a dry run until one word is changed.
* **Speed is part of the design.** The marking, the queue and a regrouping answer within a fraction of a second at the dictionary's live size, with or without table statistics: they count translations through the index on their sense, and touch each row by its id, never by a join that would keep row level security from the index. The first version took 95 seconds for a page of the queue and 243 for a regrouping, far above the 8 seconds Supabase allows a signed-in request. A test at 6,000 lemmas fails if either slow plan returns.
* **Locks, in one order.** The insert trigger of the lexicon tables reads the lemma or sense it checks `for share`: without the lock, a translation added at the moment its sense was soft deleted, by a regrouping or by an admin, could stay live under a deleted sense. A translation locks its lemma first, then its sense, the order `regroup_senses()` takes them in, which then locks the translations it reads too; in the other order an insert and a regrouping could wait for each other until PostgreSQL stopped one, and the manager reads such a stop [40P01] as a conflict, reloads and lets the person try again.

## Consequences

* The repair costs a reviewer's time: on the order of tens of hours for 5,731 lemmas, most of them [3,675] with only two senses. The keys keep each decision to a key press or two.
* A copied translation's author becomes the reviewer who regrouped it; the original row, soft deleted, keeps its author and its history.
* Translations and definitions added from outside the site [the enrichment of 0.17.0] must wait for a lemma's senses to be settled: they are keyed by sense number, which a regrouping changes. Lemmas never split, or already reviewed, are ready.
* Migration 0012 must be run before the code of 0.15.0 is pushed. It changes data: each lemma put on the list gets a new version and a line of history, credited to the SQL Editor. A backup made after it keeps the list and the review dates; one made before it restores with no review date, and its list is drawn again from the restored entries' shape, with no version raised and no line of history.
* The Users section reads any change of the review date as "significati rivisti", clearing it included: the history's summary names the fields an update changed, not their values. Clearing is done only by SQL. A kept sense that is renumbered writes two lines of history, the second from its temporary number.
* The glosses of the five chosen entries still need corrections that are content, not structure: in Italian the spoils of a sack are "bottino", not "sacco", and to sacrifice oneself is "immolarsi", not "immolare". They are made by hand in the entries.
